About the workshop

Neural language models are fluent, but they make no explicit commitment to any theory of syntax, semantics or pragmatics. Linguistic frameworks such as Categorial Grammar, HPSG, Minimalism, Construction Grammar, Dynamic Syntax and type-theoretic semantics describe exactly the structure these models leave implicit.

LingEmbed brings together NLP researchers and theoretical linguists around one question: what happens when a linguistic framework is built into a neural model as a working, differentiable component, and can rival theories be compared this way? Does theory-derived structure make models more compositional, sample-efficient, verifiable or interpretable? And when frameworks are put in the same network, which one helps on which phenomena?

You do not need to implement a whole grammar to take part. We welcome work at every level, from full implementations to probing studies, resources and negative results.

Topics

We invite submissions on topics including, but not limited to:

Grammars and theories as modules

  • categorial, Lambek or pregroup grammars compiled into tensor networks or neural parsers
  • feature structures and unification (HPSG, LFG) as neural components
  • Minimalist, construction-based or Dynamic Syntax grammars inside neural architectures
  • type-theoretic semantics (e.g. MTT, TTR) as constraints on, or components of, neural models
  • methods for compiling linguistic theories into tensors

Probing for theory

  • whether pretrained models encode the structure a theory predicts: constituency, dependency, type structure, scope
  • how that structure is distributed across the representation

Theory competition and evaluation

  • rival frameworks in the same network, compared phenomenon by phenomenon
  • benchmarks and cognitive or psycholinguistic tests of whether an embedded theory leads to human-like processing

Resources and negative results

  • treebanks, meaning representations, formalisms and grammar implementations
  • careful reports of what does not (yet) work

Complete implementations, partial or phenomenon-specific ones, theory-driven probing, and resources or negative results are all reviewed as full papers.

Task-design session

The workshop closes with an open session to design a future shared task on comparing linguistic frameworks inside neural models. We will discuss what counts as evidence that a theory helps, what must be held constant across frameworks, which phenomena and datasets to target, and which formats are realistic.

You can take part by submitting a short task-design contribution: desiderata, a pilot study, candidate evaluation items, or a feasibility analysis of an earlier shared task. The session will produce a co-authored blueprint for the next edition's shared task, and every contributor will be credited.

FIRST CALL FOR PAPERS

LingEmbed 2027
Theories Under Pressure: Embedding Linguistic Frameworks in Neural Models

First workshop, co-located with COLING 2027
Macau, China, 9–14 May 2027 (exact workshop day to be announced)
https://stergioscha.github.io/LingEmbed-workshop/

------------------------------------------------------------------------
OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Neural language models are fluent but theory-blind: they carry no
explicit commitment to any account of syntax, semantics, or pragmatics,
and it is hard to say whether a given linguistic structure does any work
inside them. Decades of linguistic theory (Categorial Grammar, HPSG,
Minimalism, Construction Grammar, Dynamic Syntax, type-theoretic
semantics) encode exactly the structure these models leave implicit.

LingEmbed asks a single question: what happens when a linguistic
framework is operationalized as a computational component inside a
neural model, and can rival theories be compared this way?

The workshop treats a linguistic theory as a callable, parameterized,
differentiable module that competes under data pressure. This turns two
communities' questions into one programme:

  * For NLP: can theory-derived structure make models more
    compositional, sample-efficient, verifiable, or interpretable?
  * For theoretical linguistics: when two frameworks compete in the same
    network, which earns its keep on which phenomena? This gives theory
    a large-scale empirical arbiter beyond introspective judgements.

Embedding a whole grammar is NOT an entry requirement. Theoretical
linguists can contribute without a GPU, and ML researchers without
committing to a framework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRACKS AND TOPICS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Grammars and theories as modules
   Full or partial operationalizations of linguistic frameworks as
   differentiable, callable components, and theory-to-tensor
   compilation schemes. For example:
   - categorial, Lambek or pregroup grammars compiled into tensor
     networks or neural parsers
   - feature structures and unification (HPSG, LFG) as neural components
   - Minimalist, construction-based or Dynamic Syntax grammars inside
     neural architectures
   - type-theoretic semantics (e.g. MTT, TTR) as constraints on or
     components of neural models

2. Probing for theory
   Testing whether pretrained models encode the structure a theory
   predicts (constituency, dependency, type structure, scope), and how
   that structure is distributed across the representation.

3. Theory competition and evaluation
   Putting rival frameworks in the same network and measuring which
   helps where. This includes benchmarks and cognitive tests of whether
   an embedded theory produces human-like processing rather than only a
   task gain.

4. Enabling resources and negative results
   Treebanks, meaning representations, formalisms and grammar
   implementations, and honest reports of what does not (yet) work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
A LADDERED CALL
------------------------------------------------------------------------

All four of the following are reviewed as full contributions:

  1. complete implementations of a framework as a differentiable module
  2. partial or phenomenon-scoped implementations
  3. theory-committed probing, with nothing compiled
  4. enabling resources or negative results

Negative results are explicitly welcome.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHARED-TASK DESIGN FORUM
------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is no shared task in this first edition. The natural task
("compile your framework and compete") cannot yet be run fairly: any
measured difference today would reflect implementation maturity rather
than theoretical adequacy.

The workshop closes instead with a structured task-design forum. It will
address what would count as evidence that a theory helps; which
comparisons are identifiable and what must be held fixed across
frameworks; which phenomena already have evaluation data and human
processing measures; and which formats are realistic. To keep the forum
concrete, we invite short task-design contributions: desiderata, pilot
studies, candidate evaluation items, and feasibility analyses of prior
shared tasks.

The forum's output is a community blueprint: a co-authored report, with
all contributors credited, specifying the format, target phenomena,
data, metrics and division of labour for the second edition's shared
task.

SUBMISSIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

We invite:

  * Archival long papers: up to 8 pages
  * Archival short papers: up to 4 pages
  * Position papers: up to 4 pages
  * Short task-design contributions for the forum: up to 2 pages
  * Non-archival submissions: work under review or published elsewhere
    (cross-submissions), in any of the formats above

Page limits exclude references and appendices. Accepted archival papers
get one additional page for the camera-ready version.

Submissions must use the official ACL style templates and be fully
anonymized for double-blind review. Reviewing is on START, with direct
submissions only (no ACL Rolling Review commitments). Archival papers
will appear in the workshop proceedings in the ACL Anthology.

Submission site: to be announced at
